Step 1

Has a password leaked?

Type a password you use. Your browser scrambles it into a 40-character code (a SHA-1 hash). Only the first 5 characters are sent to Have I Been Pwned, which returns every leaked code starting with them. Your browser does the matching, so the password never leaves this device.

Step 2

Has your email been in a breach?

For this step we send you to Have I Been Pwned, and that's on purpose. Checking an email means sending the address itself, and we'd rather never see it. Have I Been Pwned is the most widely used breach database and has been running since 2013.

  • Type your email in the search box on their home page. The free search is all you need.
  • Skip the paid plans, sponsor ads and protection offers. You don't need any of them to finish this step.
  • If breaches show up, change your password on those sites and anywhere you reused it, and turn on two-step login where it's offered.
  • "No breaches found" is good news, but not a guarantee. Keep using a different password on every site.

Step 3

Remove yourself from people-search sites

Search each site for yourself. If you're listed, follow the opt-out link. Sites marked Priority are the most widely used. Sites often relist people, so re-check anything you opted out of more than 60 days ago.

    Step 4

    Lock down the rest

    • Freeze your credit at all three bureaus. It's free and blocks new accounts in your name: Equifax, Experian, TransUnion.
    • Google "Results about you" finds search results showing your phone, address or email and lets you request removal: how it works.
    • Stop prescreened credit offers in the mail: OptOutPrescreen.com.
    • Join the Do Not Call Registry: donotcall.gov.
    • California residents can send one deletion request to 500+ registered data brokers through the state's free DROP portal.

    Opt-out links come from the volunteer-maintained Big Ass Data Broker Opt-Out List. Breach data comes from Have I Been Pwned.

    Broker sites change their forms often. If a link is broken, the site may have moved its opt-out page; search the site for "opt out" or "remove".
